Selling on credit (udhaar) is how a lot of business gets done, especially with regular customers, retailers and hotels. The problem isn't giving credit. It's keeping track of it.

A register on the counter works until the person who keeps it is away. A spreadsheet works until three people update it differently. Meanwhile money stays stuck, and asking a good customer for a payment they've already made is embarrassing.

This is a simple, reliable way to track dues.

## The three things to record

For every credit sale or payment you need just three things:

1. **Who**: the customer
2. **How much**: the amount
3. **Which way**: did they buy on credit, or pay you?

Plus, ideally, the date and payment mode (cash, UPI, bank transfer, cheque).

That's it. The balance for each customer is everything they bought on credit minus everything they paid.

## Rule 1: Record it at the moment it happens

Dues get messy when sales are written down at the end of the day from memory. The fix is to record each credit sale and payment as it happens, from whoever's phone is closest, in one shared place.

## Rule 2: Record partial payments as payments, not edits

When a customer pays ₹10,000 against a ₹25,000 bill, don't edit the original amount. Record a new payment of ₹10,000. That way you keep a clear history: what was billed, what was paid and when. That history is what settles disputes.

## Rule 3: Look at balances weekly, not when you need cash

Pick one day a week to look at:

- Customers with a balance older than your usual credit period (say 15 or 30 days)
- The biggest balances
- Anyone whose balance keeps growing

Follow up politely, with the exact amount and dates. A specific message ("₹15,000 pending from the 3 Nov order, paid ₹10,000 on 8 Nov") gets paid much faster than "please clear your dues".

## Tools you can use

**A paper register** is cheap and familiar, but only one person can use it at a time, and totals are manual.

**Ledger apps** (khata-style apps) are good at exactly this job: a balance per customer and payment reminders. If dues are your main problem, they are worth a look.

**A spreadsheet** can work: one row per transaction, a SUMIF per customer. It needs discipline and formulas, and it's awkward to update from a phone.
