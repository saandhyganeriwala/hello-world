Running out of a fast-moving item loses sales. Overstocking a slow one locks up cash. Both happen for the same reason: nobody is quite sure what's in stock right now.

Big businesses solve this with ERP systems and barcode scanners. Small shops and wholesalers usually rely on memory, a notebook or a spreadsheet updated "when there's time". Here's a lighter approach that actually stays up to date.

## Track movements, not just counts

The most common mistake is keeping only a "current stock" number and overwriting it. When it's wrong, and it will be, you have no way to find out why.

Instead, record **movements**:

- **Stock in**: purchases or production. Item, quantity, supplier, date.
- **Stock out**: sales, samples, damage. Item, quantity, customer or reason, date.

Current stock is simply everything in minus everything out. Keeping the movements means you can always trace a difference back to a date and a person.

## Keep the item list short and consistent

Inventory records fall apart when the same product has three names ("Samayi 1kg", "samayi", "Samai pack"). Decide on one name per item and stick to it. If you have hundreds of items, start with the 20 that matter most: your fastest movers and most expensive stock.

## Set a reorder level for key items

For each important item, decide the quantity at which you reorder. That's usually what you sell in the time it takes a supplier to deliver, plus a small buffer. When stock drops below that level, it's time to order.

## Count physically, on a schedule

Even with perfect recording, physical stock drifts: damage, mistakes, theft. Do a quick count of your key items weekly or monthly, compare it with your records, and record an adjustment for any difference (with a reason).

## Make recording as fast as the sale

As with orders and payments, the system only works if recording takes seconds. If staff have to find a laptop to update stock, they won't. Recording from a phone at the moment stock moves is what keeps the numbers right.

## Options

**Notebook or register**: simple, but no totals, and no visibility for anyone not standing next to it.

**Spreadsheet**: works for a small item list if one person owns it. Updating it from a phone in a busy shop is the weak point.

**Inventory or billing software**: the right choice if you invoice every sale, need GST billing, or manage hundreds of SKUs with barcodes.

## A weekly 15-minute routine

1. Ask for current stock of your top 20 items
2. Reorder anything below its reorder level
3. Physically count five items and record any adjustments
4. Look at slow movers: anything with no sales in a month?
