Ask a small business owner whether they need a CRM and the answer is usually "probably, but…". The "but" is setup: fields, pipelines, imports, training, and a monthly fee per seat for software the team might not open.

What most small teams actually need is much simpler: **stop losing leads, and know who to follow up with today.** Here's how to get that without a heavyweight CRM.

## What a minimal lead tracker needs

For each lead, record:

- **Name** (person or company) and how to reach them
- **What they want** (product, service or requirement)
- **Rough value**, even a guess
- **Stage**: New, Contacted, Qualified, Proposal, Won, Lost
- **Next step and date**: the one thing that moves it forward

That last one matters most. A lead without a next step is a lead you've already started losing.

## Run it as a pipeline

A pipeline is just your leads arranged by stage, usually as columns on a board. It answers the questions a sales team asks every day:

- How many new leads came in this week?
- Who have we not contacted yet?
- What's sitting in "Proposal" and needs a nudge?
- What's the total value of deals in progress?

Moving a lead from one column to the next takes a second, and the board stays honest.

## The real problem: keeping it updated

Every CRM fails the same way. Data entry happens later, then not at all, then the CRM is "the thing we used to use". Leads arrive on calls, WhatsApp, Instagram DMs and walk-ins, often when the salesperson is away from a computer.

So the most important feature of a lead tracker isn't reports. It's **how fast someone can record a lead from their phone, in the moment.**

## Options, from lightest to heaviest

**A shared spreadsheet**: free and flexible. Fine for a handful of leads, but stages become a text column, and nobody updates it from their phone.

**A full CRM** (HubSpot, Zoho, Salesforce and others): powerful, with email tracking, automation and reports. Worth it when sales is a dedicated function with a manager who'll maintain it.

**A chat-first tracker** like [CRUD.chat](/): somewhere in between. You record leads by message and get a real pipeline view, without setup.

## How it works in CRUD.chat

Anyone on the team sends a message the way they'd tell a colleague:

- "Riya wants wholesale dry fruit boxes, around 40k. Call her back Monday"
- "Grand Hotel interested in wedding hampers, 2.5 lakh, sent proposal"
- "Sunil, corporate Diwali gifts, 80k, contacted"

Each becomes a lead with name, interest, value and stage. Then:

- **Ask for a pipeline:** "show leads as a pipeline with totals" gives you a board with a column per stage and the value of each.
- **Move leads** by dragging cards, or by message: "Grand Hotel won".
- **Ask questions:** "which leads haven't been contacted?", "total value in proposal stage?"
- **Create follow-ups:** "remind Pranav to call Riya tomorrow" adds a task with a due date.
- **Keep customers too.** Leads, customers, orders and payments live in the same workspace, so a won lead can turn into an order without retyping.

It's not trying to be Salesforce. It's for the team that would otherwise use a spreadsheet, or nothing.

## Start with one week

Don't migrate everything. For the next seven days, record every new lead the moment it comes in, and every stage change as it happens. At the end of the week, look at the pipeline. You'll see leads you would have forgotten.
