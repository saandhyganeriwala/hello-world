For many small businesses, WhatsApp *is* the order system. Customers send a message, a voice note or a photo of what they want. Staff forward it to a group. Somebody notes it down, maybe. Then the questions start: Was that order packed? Did they pay the advance? Which address did they say?

WhatsApp is a great way to *receive* orders. It is a poor way to *keep* them. Messages scroll away, details are spread across chats, and there's no way to see all open orders at once.

Here is a simple system that keeps WhatsApp as the front door but gives you a proper order record behind it.

## Step 1: Decide what an order record contains

Before any tool, agree on the few details every order needs. For most businesses that's:

- **Customer** (name, and phone if it's a new customer)
- **Items and quantity**
- **Amount**, and whether an advance was paid
- **Delivery** details: address or city, and date
- **Status**: New, Confirmed, Packing, Dispatched, Delivered
- **Payment**: Pending, Advance paid, Paid

Keep it short. Every extra field is one more reason for someone to skip recording.

## Step 2: Record every order within a minute of receiving it

The biggest cause of lost orders isn't the tool. It's delay. An order noted "later" is an order half-remembered.

Make the rule simple: **whoever receives the order records it immediately**, from their phone, in one place the whole team can see. That only works if recording is as quick as forwarding a message.

## Step 3: Use statuses, not memory

Each order should move through statuses as work happens: New → Confirmed → Packing → Dispatched → Delivered. A board or pipeline view, with one column per status, shows at a glance what's waiting, what's in progress and what's stuck.

When the packer finishes, they move the order (or send "Kashish order packed"). When the delivery person drops it off, they mark it delivered. Nobody has to ask in the group.

## Step 4: Track payments against orders

Advance paid, balance pending and payment mode should live on the order or in a linked payments list. Then "how much is pending from Hotel Orchid?" has an answer without scrolling through chats and bank SMS.

## Step 5: Review open orders every day

Take two minutes each morning:

- Orders still **New** after a day: did someone confirm them?
- Orders **Dispatched** but not delivered after the expected date
- **Delivered** orders with payment still pending

## Doing it in a spreadsheet vs. a chat-first tool
